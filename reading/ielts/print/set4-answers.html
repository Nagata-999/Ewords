Passage 1 · 20 minutes · Questions 1–13

The Productive Uses of Uncertainty

1. FALSE

A precise single number is always more informative than a range.

Evidence: a single precise number ... can create an illusion of accuracy

精密さが常に情報価値を高めるとは述べていません。

2. TRUE

Model results depend partly on assumptions.

Evidence: the output depends on assumptions

本文と一致します。

3. FALSE

All uncertainty disappears when more data are collected.

Evidence: without making it disappear

すべて消えるとは否定されています。

4. TRUE

Decision-makers sometimes act before certainty is complete.

Evidence: cannot wait for complete certainty

本文の言い換えです。

5. FALSE

A shaded visual area is guaranteed to show probability accurately.

Evidence: may suggest ... even when this has not been established

guaranteed と矛盾します。

6. TRUE

Uncertainty can be manipulated to support political goals.

Evidence: exaggerate uncertainty ... or minimise it

本文と一致します。

Questions 7–13

Complete the summary. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage for each answer.

Uncertainty provides information about limits and 7. reliability. Instruments have limited 8. precision, while models simplify reality. Some uncertainty reflects genuine 9. variability and cannot be removed. Engineers respond by using safety 10. margins. Communication should separate the probable from the merely 11. possible. Groups may magnify uncertainty to delay 12. regulation. Honest uncertainty allows plans to 13. adapt as evidence changes.

8. precision

9. variability

10. margins

11. possible

12. regulation

13. adapt

Passage 2 · 20 minutes · Questions 14–26

Smell and the Architecture of Memory

14. FALSE

Odour-evoked memories are always factually accurate.

Evidence: a powerful recollection can still contain errors

always accurate と errors が矛盾します。

15. TRUE

People may recognise a smell without being able to name it.

Evidence: recognise that a smell is familiar while being unable to name it

本文と一致します。

16. TRUE

Labels can alter whether an identical smell seems pleasant.

Evidence: judgments of pleasantness can change

本文の言い換えです。

17. FALSE

Every culture interprets the smell of cleanliness in the same way.

Evidence: in one community ... in another

文化によって異なります。

18. TRUE

Commercial products can contribute to shared smell references.

Evidence: create shared references

本文と一致します。

19. FALSE

Museums can reconstruct historical smells with complete certainty.

Evidence: Reconstructing an authentic past odour is difficult

complete certainty と矛盾します。

Questions 20–26

Complete the summary. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage for each answer.

Smell signals reach areas related to emotion and 20. memory. Intensity does not guarantee factual 21. reliability. Naming an odour can organise perception but may also 22. distort it. Culture affects exposure and 23. interpretation. Museum reconstructions rely partly on subjective written 24. descriptions. The smell of books comes from compounds released as paper 25. deteriorates. Odour is shaped by social 26. frameworks, not only chemistry.

21. reliability

22. distort

23. interpretation

24. descriptions
Written descriptions are subjective
25. deteriorates

26. frameworks

Passage 3 · 20 minutes · Questions 27–40

When Efficiency Creates Fragility

27. FALSE

Efficient systems always respond well to unusual disturbances.

Evidence: until an unusual disturbance exposes how little room they have

always と本文が矛盾します。

28. TRUE

Resources that support resilience may appear wasteful during normal periods.

Evidence: features that appear inefficient

本文と一致します。

29. FALSE

Just-in-time supply necessarily fails in every industry.

Evidence: If transport is reliable, the system works well

necessarily fails を否定できます。

30. TRUE

Hospitals can use flexible resources instead of maintaining all emergency capacity permanently.

Evidence: Some systems therefore maintain flexible resources

本文と一致します。

31. TRUE

Performance targets can change worker behaviour without improving the service.

Evidence: improve the score ... without improving the underlying service

本文の言い換えです。

32. FALSE

Identical backups in one location always provide strong protection.

Evidence: offers little protection

strong protection と little protection が矛盾します。

33. FALSE

The passage recommends the same reserve capacity for every service.

Evidence: essential needs may justify higher reserves

サービスにより異なると述べています。

Questions 34–40

Complete the summary. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage for each answer.

Efficiency removes waste but may leave little room to respond to a 34. disturbance. Resilience can depend on equipment or resources that remain 35. idle. Just-in-time systems reduce warehouses and tied-up 36. capital. Public services may use buildings that can be 37. converted. Indicators tend to favour what is easy to 38. count. Effective redundancy requires 39. diversity, not merely identical backups. Decisions about reserves distribute costs and future 40. risks.

36. capital

37. converted

38. count

39. diversity
Diversity matters
40. risks
